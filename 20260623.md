"Tokenmaxxing" is a sharp signal of ROI failure. Coinbase, Walmart, Amazon, and others have shifted from incentivizing maximum AI usage to imposing hard caps after costs outran output (Business Insider). Pylon's CEO disclosed that their Anthropic plan costs would triple at 150 employees without intervention. The word "Tokens" appeared in 129 Q2 2026 earnings calls, up from 57 the prior quarter (Business Insider). Sam Altman acknowledged AI spending is now "a huge issue" for enterprise customers. Average per-employee AI spend hit $66.29 in May, still rising even as caps are being set (Business Insider). The gap most mid-market companies lack — AI spend governance and ROI measurement frameworks — is now a named C-suite problem, not a theoretical one.
States push for more laws to curb bias and improve transparency, despite federal posture. Colorado in May required companies that deploy AI systems in important areas such as employment, education, housing or banking to tell people when it’s being used to influence a decision about them. Connecticut, Washington and Utah required AI developers to embed data into digital content that will allow users to determine whether the content — such as photos or video — has been created or altered by AI. In California, lawmakers are advancing the “No Robo Bosses Act of 2026” to prohibit employers from relying solely on AI to fire or discipline workers, and to expand how the state regulates AI chatbots, including banning the use of chatbot outputs to children for advertising.
AI job displacement data is more nuanced than headlines suggest. The ECB published a methodologically grounded study finding aggregate U.S. employment and wage effects from AI remain muted through 2025 (Reuters). However, high-AI-substitution-risk jobs (e.g., economists, graphic designers) declined by over 4% from 2019–2025, while low-risk jobs grew by 13%. Separately, Challenger, Gray & Christmas data shows 87,714 AI-attributed layoffs so far in 2026, already exceeding all of 2025's 54,836 — with Meta, Microsoft, and Oracle named (Forbes). Caveat: Challenger counts self-reported company rationale, not verified causation. The ECB data is the more defensible counterpoint when conversations conflate layoff announcements with structural job destruction.
